    Clipward

    Small app, careful defaults

    Clipward stays out of the way in the menu bar. Everything it does to protect what you copy is switched on from the first launch.

    Clipward

    Pasted items don't linger

    When you paste something from your history, Clipward takes it back off the clipboard a minute later, so a token you pasted once isn't sitting there for the next app to read. If you copy something new in the meantime, that stays.

    Encrypted on disk, key in your Keychain

    Every item is stored with AES-256 and an HMAC-SHA256 seal, in files only your user account can read, excluded from Time Machine. The key lives in your login Keychain. If the Keychain isn't available, history stays in memory and is never written as plain text.

    Passwords are never recorded

    Password managers mark the passwords they copy as concealed. Clipward respects that marker, so copies from 1Password, Bitwarden, KeePassXC, the Passwords app and others never reach your history. You can also ignore any app entirely, or pause recording from the menu bar.

    Comes back exactly as you copied it

    Clipward keeps every format an app puts on the clipboard, not just the text. Formatted text keeps its formatting, images stay images, and copied files paste as files. Hold ⇧ when you paste to drop the formatting.

    Install in a minute

    1. Download

      Get the latest DMG from GitHub releases.

    2. Drag to Applications

      Open the DMG and drag Clipward into your Applications folder.

    3. Open it once

      Clipward isn't notarized by Apple yet. The first time, open it, then go to System Settings › Privacy & Security and click Open Anyway. On macOS 14 and earlier, right-click the app and choose Open.

    To paste straight into other apps, allow Clipward under Privacy & Security › Accessibility. Without it, Clipward copies the item and you press ⌘V yourself.
